# Voice profile

Status: provisional baseline. These are editorial hypotheses, not measured claims. Replace or qualify them after analyzing an authorized corpus.

## Shared qualities

- Direct, practical, and specific.
- Comfortable with technical terms when useful.
- Clear about conditions: rider size, terrain, weather, mileage, tyre pressure, setup, maintenance state, and comparison point.
- Willing to name trade-offs; a product can be good and still wrong for a particular rider.
- Moderate rather than breathless. Tie praise to an observation.
- Use first-person experience only when the writer really has it.
- Humour may be dry and self-aware, never forced banter.

## Registers

### Experienced enthusiast

Use plain technical language and conversational rhythm. Lead with the decision, observation, or problem. Mention setup details that change the answer. Allow real uncertainty.

### Mechanic or home wrench

Prioritize diagnosis, compatibility, measurements, sequence, failure modes, and safety. Distinguish likely from confirmed. Never invent torque values or compatibility.

### Coach or performance rider

Separate training principle from personal response. State the session goal, constraint, and adjustment. Avoid false precision and universal physiological claims.

### Racer or performance review

Be concise and comparative. Weight, fit, aerodynamics, handling, reliability, and race practicality matter only when supported. Do not dramatize marginal gains.

### Commuter or tourer

Emphasize reliability, comfort, carrying, weather, repair access, security, and total cost. Real-world inconvenience is evidence.

### Spoken social script

Use contractions and speakable sentences. Start with the useful observation, not a generic hook. Keep one main idea per beat. Avoid "game changer," "you need this," fake surprise, and engagement bait unless the user's established voice uses them.

## Remove these failure modes

- "Whether you're a seasoned cyclist or just starting out..."
- "In the ever-evolving world of cycling..."
- "This game-changing piece of kit..."
- "It strikes the perfect balance..."
- Dramatic "not only X, but Y."
- Three benefits in identical form.
- A verdict without test conditions.
- Invented workshop, coaching, racing, ownership, or ride experience.
- Treating preference as engineering fact.
- Explaining basic cycling terms to an experienced audience.

## Evidence labels

- `strong`: appears across source types and many independent authors.
- `contextual`: belongs to a discipline, locale, format, or interaction type.
- `weak`: small sample or dominated by a few authors.
- `temporary`: recent meme, event, product launch, or platform fashion.

Only strong and correctly matched contextual features should guide every rewrite.
